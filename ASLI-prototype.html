DIGITAL SHRAM SANKALP / PROBLEM STATEMENT 04

Outreach and fraud
are the same problem.

Fraudulent job portals do not succeed because they are sophisticated. They succeed because a worker holding a job advertisement has no cheap way to answer one question: is this real?

Outreach that raises interest without supplying an answer creates demand that fraudsters harvest. Awareness without a verification primitive is a subsidy to the fraud economy.

Why takedown will always lose

Enforcement against fraudulent employment platforms is necessarily retrospective. A fake state staff selection website ran on a third-party domain, collected registration fees through QR codes, and was removed from search results only after the operator was arrested. Every victim had already paid before the takedown began.

Always
after the payment
Detection, reporting, investigation and takedown all follow the loss. The money has usually already moved.
Minutes

A third-party domain, a copied layout and a QR code. The cost of creating the next fake site is near zero.

Awareness and fraud prevention are budgeted and run separately. They are the same intervention.

What everyone else will propose

and why it plateaus

A classifier that crawls the web for phishing domains impersonating government portals, plus a public awareness campaign with posters and social media.

Both are worth doing. Neither scales. Detection capacity is fixed and the supply of new domains is not. Campaign reach is bought and decays the moment the budget stops.

And crucially, a campaign that tells a worker to be careful without telling them how to check just makes them anxious, not safe.

What ASLI proposes instead

one primitive, deployed once

Make authenticity checkable at the point of contact. Every genuine NCS vacancy carries a short signed code. Any worker, anywhere, through any channel, can ask whether the advertisement in front of them corresponds to a real vacancy.

Takedown scales with enforcement capacity, which is fixed. Verification scales with the number of people who ask, which is not.

Outreach then has something to distribute: not a slogan, but a habit.

Case reference: publicly reported enforcement action against a fraudulent state staff selection commission website hosted on a third-party domain, collecting registration fees via QR code, taken down after arrests.

Mohar: the vacancy carries its own proof

When an employer posts a genuine vacancy on NCS, the system issues a short signed verification code and a QR alongside it. The code is designed to survive the journey an advertisement actually takes: photographed, forwarded on WhatsApp, printed on a poster, read aloud over a phone.

VACANCY NOTICE

Kaveri Precision Components
Machine Operator · 12 positions · Hosur
Rs 16,500 per month
NCS-7K4M-92

Scan or send this code to 14434 to check if this job is real

The code is short enough to be read over a phone call, spoken to an IVR line, or typed by someone with limited literacy. That constraint drives the design: eight characters, no ambiguous glyphs, checksum built in.

Why a signed code and not a link

A link can be cloned. A code cannot be guessed.

Fraudulent sites already copy government layouts and domain patterns convincingly. A cryptographically signed code resolves only against the authoritative register, so a copied poster with a fabricated code returns "no such vacancy" rather than a convincing forgery.

It works where the worker actually is

The overwhelming majority of job leads reach informal workers through a forward, a wall, or a person, not through a portal. The code travels with the advertisement into all of those channels without needing an app.

Absence of a code is itself informative

Once codes are ubiquitous, an advertisement without one becomes conspicuous. The verification habit does the work that a takedown pipeline cannot.

Poochh: one question, four channels, twenty-two languages

The verification above runs on WhatsApp, plain SMS, a missed call that triggers an IVR callback, and a QR scan. Every reply carries the same closing sentence, because a fee demand is the single most reliable fraud signal available and it costs nothing to repeat.

Worker sees an advertisement

A WhatsApp forward, a poster at a labour chowk, a consultancy office wall, a Telegram group, a newspaper insert.

→

Sends the code, or the evidence

The code where one exists. Where none does, the website address, the phone number or the UPI handle being used to collect money.

→

Gets an answer, not a lecture

Whether the vacancy exists, who posted it, the role, the wage, and the fee rule. In their own language, in seconds, with no login.

Where no code exists, give a risk assessment, not a verdict

Most advertisements a worker encounters will not carry a code, at least at first. Sending a URL, phone number or UPI handle returns a graded answer: whether that identifier has been reported before, whether the domain was registered last week, whether the UPI handle is linked to other reports. An honest "we cannot confirm this, and here is what is unusual about it" is more useful than silence, and far more useful than a false reassurance.

The sentence that does most of the work

Government employment services never charge a fee. Any demand for money is fraud.

It appears in every reply, in every language, whether the answer is yes or no. A worker who internalises only this one rule is protected against the large majority of employment fraud, including every variant the system has never seen before. This is the cheapest line of defence available and it requires no detection capability whatsoever.

Jaal: a report is a node, not a ticket

Every report carries identifiers: a domain, a phone number, a UPI handle, a bank account. Handled as individual complaints, they resolve one case each. Clustered, they resolve operators. Click a node to see what it links.

What clustering changes

One report protects the next thousand

A single report identifying a UPI handle immediately marks every other advertisement using that handle, including ones nobody has reported yet. The marginal value of a report rises with the size of the graph rather than falling.

Enforcement receives a network, not a pile

The cluster, with its shared payment rails and contact numbers, is handed to the national cybercrime reporting portal and the 1930 financial fraud helpline. A payment rail can be frozen far faster than a domain can be taken down.

The fee demand is the join key

Fraudulent operations must eventually collect money, and collection leaves an identifier. That is the structural weakness in the whole fraud economy, and it is where a verification system should aim.

Reports are pseudonymous by default. A worker does not have to identify themselves to warn others. Requiring identification to report fraud suppresses exactly the reports the system most needs, particularly from workers who are embarrassed at having nearly paid.

Graph is synthetic and constructed to demonstrate clustering behaviour. A production build shares clusters with the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal and the 1930 helpline under agreed protocols.

Outreach where the queue already is

Workers do not visit employment portals. They visit places where they already have a reason to stand in line. Outreach should be distribution of the verification habit through that existing footfall, not the purchase of attention through campaigns.

Fair price shops

Nearly every unorganised worker household collects rations. A printed verification card at the counter reaches more workers per rupee than any digital channel, and the shopkeeper already explains things to people.

existing footfall

Common Service Centres

Already the assisted channel for eShram registration and correction. The operator can demonstrate a verification once, and the worker keeps the habit.

existing footfall

Bank business correspondents

The BC point is where the money actually moves. A verification prompt at the moment of a cash transfer is the closest intervention to the point of loss.

existing footfall

Model Career Centres

407 approved centres, including 370 upgraded Employment Exchanges, already exist for exactly this audience and already run job fairs.

existing network

Panchayat offices and labour chowks

The labour chowk is where informal hiring physically happens. A painted code on the wall is permanent infrastructure at the cost of paint.

existing footfall

The forward itself

Every verification reply ends with a line the worker can forward to whoever sent them the advertisement. The channel that carries the fraud also carries the correction.

zero marginal cost

This is the part that makes outreach and fraud prevention one budget line rather than two. Every rupee spent distributing the verification habit reduces fraud losses and increases genuine portal usage at the same time, because the worker who checks a fake advertisement learns where the real ones are.

Three modules

One verification primitive, delivered through channels that already exist, feeding an intelligence graph that already has a home in national enforcement infrastructure.

Moharthe signed vacancy

Every genuine NCS vacancy is issued a short signed verification code and QR at the moment of posting. Eight characters, no ambiguous glyphs, checksum built in, designed to be read aloud over a phone or typed by someone with limited literacy. The code resolves only against the authoritative register.

Poochhask from anywhere

WhatsApp, SMS, missed-call IVR and QR scan, in 22 languages through Bhashini. No login, no personal data, no app. Where no code exists, a URL, phone number or UPI handle returns a graded risk assessment rather than a verdict. Every reply carries the fee rule.

Jaalthe fraud network

Reports are clustered on shared identifiers into operators rather than incidents. Clusters are shared with the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal and the 1930 helpline, where a payment rail can be frozen faster than a domain can be removed.

Safeguards and measurement

A verification service used by vulnerable people at a moment of financial anxiety has to be designed so that using it costs the worker nothing, including in privacy.

No login, no personal data

A code in, an answer out. Nobody should have to identify themselves to find out whether a job advertisement is real.

Pseudonymous reporting

Reports carry the fraudster's identifiers, not the reporter's. Requiring identification suppresses exactly the reports the graph needs most.

Never a false all-clear

Where a vacancy cannot be confirmed, the reply says so plainly. The system never manufactures reassurance it cannot support.

Employers are not exposed

A verification reply confirms the vacancy, the role and the wage as posted. It does not disclose applicant data or anything the employer did not publish.

Graded, not binary, on unknowns

An unrecognised domain is reported as unrecognised, with what is unusual about it, rather than labelled fraudulent without evidence.

Reports published in aggregate

Fee-demand incidents are reported monthly. Fraud volume that is not published cannot be managed by anyone outside the system.
